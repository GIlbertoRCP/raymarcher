Physics Guide

Gravitational Lensing: The strong gravitational field of the black hole warps spacetime, forcing photons to follow curved paths. Light from behind the black hole is focused toward the observer, producing visual double images, distorted arches, and circular Einstein Rings.

Accretion Disk: Gas and dust orbiting the black hole form a thin, spinning disk. Because light is lensed, you can see both the top and bottom of the rear side of the disk, warped into halos that wrap around the event horizon.

Doppler Beaming: The side of the accretion disk rotating toward you appears significantly brighter and hotter (blueshifted) due to relativistic velocity. The side rotating away is dimmer and redder (redshifted).

Frame Dragging: A rotating (Kerr) black hole drags the very fabric of spacetime around with it. This breaks the spherical symmetry of the lensing and accretion disk, shifting the photon sphere and horizon shape relative to the spin axis.
